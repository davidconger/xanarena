# Xanarena: Rush easter egg ideas

These are the Rush easter egg ideas. Each one is written for true fans, the band and the crew to catch, and each explains the real reference so it can be checked before anything ships.

**Status:** concepts 1, 1B and 1C all have these eggs working: the YYZ light, the 21:12 counter, the song-title on-screen text ("Down the tubes tour" and the CC button included), the secret codes and the backline credit. Concept 1C (`concept-1c-deep-cuts/`) also has "Distant early warning" and "Rated R40". Everything else is mocked up in the lab at `easter-eggs/index.html`, including the 404 page at `easter-eggs/404.html`.

## In the player (concepts 1 and 1B)

### Tape counter stops at 21:12
- **Where:** the VCR's LCD counter, when the tape ends or on pause.
- **Why it works:** *2112* (1976) is the album that saved their career. Here it's just a number on a VCR display, so only fans will notice.

### Power light blinks Y-Y-Z in Morse code
- **Where:** the VCR's power or record LED, looping quietly.
- **Pattern:** `-.--  -.--  --..`
- **Why it works:** YYZ is the code for Toronto Pearson airport. The song "YYZ" (*Moving Pictures*, 1981) opens with that Morse rhythm. Anyone who has played it will recognize the blink.
- **Note:** the "Gate YYZ" on the flyer 3 ticket already uses this reference. The blinking light gives it motion.

### Warning card titled "Distant early warning"
- **Where:** the pre-roll warning card in concept 1, in place of the generic warning heading.
- **Why it works:** "Distant early warning" is from *Grace Under Pressure* (1984), the same year as the concept 1 basement. The title comes from the DEW Line, a chain of Cold War radar stations across the Canadian Arctic. It's a Canadian reference used as a warning screen.

### Button labels as song titles
- **Where:** the on-screen display text when deck buttons are pressed.
- **Ideas:**
  - Pause: "Time stand still" (*Hold Your Fire*, 1987)
  - Fast forward: "Fly by night" (*Fly by Night*, 1975)
- **Why it works:** VCRs always flashed a word on screen, so swapping in a song title fits.
- **Restraint:** use two, not five. Rewind stays "Rewind" because "Be kind, rewind" carries the whole concept.

### Bad tracking shows "Down the tubes tour"
- **Where:** the message that appears when tracking goes wrong.
- **Why it works:** that was the band's own nickname for the 1975 to 1976 *Caress of Steel* tour. The album sold poorly and they played small rooms. It's a self-deprecating joke from their history, and the band and longtime crew will know it.

### Subtitle menu: "English, Canadian, Blah blah blah"
- **Where:** a subtitle or language option on the deck or in the menu.
- **Why it works:** the box already lists "English, Canadian, Troll". Adding "Blah blah blah" points to Alex's 2013 Rock and Roll Hall of Fame speech, where he gave his whole speech as "blah blah blah".

### Optional: secret code
- **Where:** typing `YYZ` or `2112` on the page.
- **What it does:** plays the Morse beeps as a generated tone, or briefly shows a nod on the TV.
- **Note:** use a generated tone, not the song audio. Don't use the official Starman artwork (see Guardrails).

## In the 1B night scene

### Guitar case stamped "Property of the Temples of Syrinx"
- **Where:** a stencil on the guitar case the walker drops.
- **Why it works:** in the *2112* story, a man finds a forgotten guitar and brings it to the Priests of the Temples of Syrinx, who smash it because they control all music. In 1B the case gets dropped and something gets out, a fitting comeback for that guitar.

### Dalmatian sniffing a fire hydrant
- **Where:** under the street lamp in the night scene.
- **Why it works:** that's the cover of *Signals* (1982). On its own it's a dog on a street, and fans will recognize the cover.

### An owl among the crows
- **Where:** one more bird on the wire, with eyes that blink at a different time from the crows.
- **Why it works:** *Fly by Night* (1975) has an owl on the cover. It also fits a scene set at night.

## Credits, back of the box and flyers

### Backline credit
- **Where:** the concept 3 credit roll, or the credits on the back of the concept 2 box.
- **Copy idea:** "Backline: two dryers, a vending machine, a rotisserie and a sausage maker."
- **Why it works:** Geddy replaced his amp stacks with stage props:
  - clothes dryers on the *Vapor Trails* tour (2002)
  - two dryers and a vending machine on *R30* (2004)
  - rotisserie chicken ovens on *Snakes & Arrows* (2007 to 2008)
  - a sausage maker on the *Time Machine* tour (2010 to 2011)

  The crew who carried those props will catch this first.

### "Rated R40" badge
- **Where:** the rating box on the back of the box or on a flyer.
- **Why it works:** R30 and R40 were their 30th and 40th anniversary tours. It reads as a movie rating.

### Fake review from "Bytor and the Snow Dog"
- **Where:** next to the existing fake pull quotes ("Kubla Klan").
- **Why it works:** "By-Tor and the Snow Dog" is the long, epic track on *Fly by Night* (1975). Putting the characters in a review credit fits the joke the box is already making.

### Anika Nilles credited as "Die Schlagzeugerin"
- **Where:** the credit roll or the cast line.
- **Why it works:** Neil's drum solos were named "The Drummer" in different languages:
  - "O Baterista" on *Rush in Rio*
  - "Der Trommler" on *R30*
  - "De Slagwerker" on *Snakes & Arrows Live*

  Anika is German, and "Schlagzeugerin" is the feminine form of drummer. It welcomes her by carrying on Neil's naming tradition.

### "Moving pictures" error page
- **Where:** a 404 page.
- **Copy idea:** movers carrying a framed picture, with "This page has been moved."
- **Why it works:** the *Moving Pictures* (1981) cover is a triple pun: movers carrying paintings, onlookers who are emotionally moved, and it's an album named after movies. A missing page is literally something that got moved.

## Guardrails

- **Neil:** keep any nod light and celebratory, for example a single "Thanks, Professor" line in the credits, and run it by the band's team first.
- **Copyright:** no lyric quotes and no official artwork, including the Starman logo and the real album covers. Song titles, poses and wordplay are fine.
- **Volume:** pick a handful. A few well-placed eggs feel like a wink; too many become a trivia quiz.
